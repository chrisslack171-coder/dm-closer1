# Landing Page Checklist + Template
Distilled from John Whiting's "Landing Pages That Sell Without Sales Calls" course, adapted for a $27 low-ticket offer sold to beginner creators — with your proof (sales psychology, Golden Door Award, 1,000+ D2D closes) baked into the template slots.

How to use: Part 1 is the audit — run it on any page before launch and every time you change something. Part 2 is the build template — copy it into a Google Doc and fill the brackets, THEN design. (Whiting mocks everything in a doc first: copy decides the design, never the reverse.)

---

## PART 1 — THE PRE-LAUNCH CHECKLIST

### A. Above the fold (mobile first — always audit on your phone)
- [ ] **Peripheral-vision test passed**: look at a point beside your phone — can you still read the headline's promise? If you have to focus, it fails.
- [ ] Headline is **~10 words**, leads with the OUTCOME the reader wants and doesn't have
- [ ] Numbers/timeframes are **believable ranges**, not fantasy ("your first 10 sales" beats "$10K months")
- [ ] Headline speaks **one tier above** your actual buyer (you attract below the level you speak to)
- [ ] Contains a **new-opportunity signal**: "new," "install," the current year, or a named mechanism
- [ ] The word **"easy" appears nowhere** ("easy attracts shitty people" — use "simple")
- [ ] **CTA button fully visible** above the fold — not half-cut by the screen edge
- [ ] Something **induces the scroll** (a partially visible element, an arrow, a face peeking up)
- [ ] Promise is framed as **"my goal is" / "could,"** never a guarantee ("will" triggers skeptics)

### B. Copy (the skim test)
- [ ] Read ONLY the bold/large text top to bottom — **does it tell the complete story alone?** Your reader is a skimmer on the toilet.
- [ ] At least one **copy hook** sells the next unit of attention ("Take 90 seconds to read this…" / "P.S. Read the rest of this page")
- [ ] **Open loops** exist (an unexplained screenshot, an unnamed result, a "what link?" moment) — and the payoff comes later, not immediately
- [ ] The problem is **articulated in the buyer's own words**, better than they could say it themselves
- [ ] Pain led with is the one **on their mind right now**; the secondary benefit is sub-stitched, not the headline
- [ ] **One ask per page.** Count your CTAs. If there's more than one destination, delete until there's one. "Funnels don't have holes in the side."
- [ ] CTA is **named after the desire**, not your deliverable ("Yes, I want my first sale" — never "Get the PDF")
- [ ] Every extra element removed — each additional piece of information is another chance to object

### C. Proof
- [ ] Testimonials **ordered by recognizability**, names displayed smallest
- [ ] **One curated quote per card**, hitting a specific desire/pain in ICP language, all cards identical in size
- [ ] Only testimonials **at or above the level you want to attract** ("display zero-to-heroes and you will attract zeros" — for a beginner offer this means: show believable FIRST wins, not lottery outcomes)
- [ ] Each testimonial works **as the only one watched/read**
- [ ] Not over-proving — a curated few beats a wall ("too many creates more doubt")
- [ ] Photos are **you WITH people**, never just their headshot; expressions model the emotion you're selling
- [ ] Personal imagery: **family/real life over flexes** ("babies convert"; watches attract the wrong people)
- [ ] **Disclaimers placed high**, not buried — stated risk builds trust

### D. Visual hierarchy
- [ ] **Color coding is consistent**: one color = pain (red/dark), one = goal (green/light), one = your mechanism — and never crossed (don't circle a win in red)
- [ ] **Old Way / New Way graphic**: old way NAMED something bad, cramped, ~10 steps, gritty font; new way half the steps, whitespace, clean font — works even if nobody reads it
- [ ] Identity choice uses the **Rule of Three** (two's not enough, four's too many), with the question "Which do you want to be?"
- [ ] **Circles and arrows** direct the eye to exactly what matters
- [ ] **Title case headlines, normal-case body, zero all-caps** ("all caps is yelling")
- [ ] Something **moves** (autoplay video, scrolling strip) — movement creates engagement
- [ ] Page doesn't look like every AI-template page in your niche — visible craft signals you didn't mail it in

### E. Trust (Broken Windows audit)
- [ ] **Every link clicked, every video played, every image loaded** — on mobile AND desktop. One broken thing = "what else is wrong?"
- [ ] Ad → page **congruence**: the page continues the exact promise of the ad/post that sent them
- [ ] A **legitimacy signal** exists (support contact, real footer, phone number if low-ticket)
- [ ] You've **googled/ChatGPT'd yourself** as a prospect would — nothing findable contradicts the page
- [ ] Prices, dates, and claims are current everywhere they appear

### F. Funnel discipline
- [ ] This page's job is ONE of: capture / convert / consume — and nothing on it serves a different job
- [ ] Retargeting content has **no conversion buttons** — middle of funnel is for consumption ("not convert, not convert")
- [ ] You're not building above your level on the complexity ladder (DM funnel = 2, book-a-call = 3, SLO = 10 — "if you can't dial in a two, you're above your head")
- [ ] Split tests change **one element** in A/B; a whole new ANGLE gets its own campaign
- [ ] Anything that loses to the control **dies**, no matter how much you like it — "the actual result is senior to anything"
- [ ] Launch bar met: "I don't see how this couldn't do pretty well" — meticulous, then shipped. Not perfect, then stale.

## PART 2 — THE FILL-IN-THE-BLANKS PAGE TEMPLATE
The Whiting SLO anatomy in order, sized for a $27 offer. Fill every [bracket] in a doc first. Sections marked (optional) can be cut for a shorter page — cut from the bottom of each zone, never the top.

### Zone 1: The Hook (above the fold)
1. **Headline:** [Believable result] in [believable timeframe] without [the thing they hate]
   - Safe control version: "Install the [named system] that [outcome] — so you can [payoff] in [year]"
   - Your flavor: "The [X]-message system that turns comments into $27 sales — without feeling like a salesperson"
2. **Sub-headline (contrast color):** without [obstacle #2 — the objection they'd raise first]
3. **CTA button:** "Yes, I want [the desire]" — visible without scrolling
4. **Scroll inducer:** [your photo/element peeking from below the fold + arrow]

### Zone 2: The Letter
5. **"From the desk of [Your Name]"** + copy hook: "Take 90 seconds to read this. You'll be glad you did."
6. **Proof block FIRST:** [screenshot of a real result — DM thread ending in a sale, sales dashboard — key number circled in your GOAL color] + honest disclaimer directly under it
7. **Anti-positioning line:** "I'm not a [guru category they're numb to]." — Yours: "I'm not an internet marketing guru. I closed 1,000+ sales face-to-face at strangers' doors."
8. **Story in skim-headlines** (bold lines alone must carry it):
   - "[Year]: [the low point in one line]"
   - "Then I [turning point / mechanism discovery]"
   - "Now [current desirable state, specific]"
   - [Real signature image]
   - "P.S. Read the rest of this page — [open loop]"

### Zone 3: The Offer
9. **"Introducing [Product Name]"** + clean product mockup [not an obvious template]
10. **The [Product] Effect — skim-line benefits:** 3-5 bold one-liners: "[objection] evaporates" / "[hard thing] becomes optional" / "[desire] shows up ready to buy"
11. **Old Way / New Way graphic:**
    - Old way name: "[Something that sounds exhausting — e.g., 'Beg-and-Chase Selling']" — ~10 cramped steps in pain color
    - New way name: "[Your mechanism]" — 3-4 clean steps, whitespace
12. **"Imagine this" future-pace:** [the dream notification/DM, written verbatim as they'd receive it]
13. **Three simple steps:** Step 1 [know], Step 2 [create/do — set honest expectations: "helps you," not "does it for you"], Step 3 [receive]

### Zone 4: The Close
14. **Bonuses (3-6), each named as a thing they NEED:** "[The ___ Optimizer]", "[The ___ Checklist]", "[The 98 ___]" — each solves the next objection in line
15. **Identity graphic (Rule of Three):** [The Ghost] / [The Wantrepreneur-equivalent] / [The identity you sell] + "Which one do you want to be?" (optional)
16. **Recap:** "Everything you're getting today" list + headline restated + price + "$27." (no "only" — "only" tells them YOU think it's not worth it)
17. **Risk reversal badge under EVERY buy button:** [guarantee, stated plainly]
18. **FAQ (5-7 real objections)** + 2-3 final testimonials — deliberately few

### Post-purchase (the Whiting move that pays twice)
19. The proof screenshots you used in Zone 2 should **pre-frame your next offer** — if they saw a clean dashboard/system they don't have, the upsell is already half-sold.

## PART 3 — THE BEST THINGS I LEARNED FROM THIS COURSE

1. **The peripheral-vision test is the single best 5-second audit that exists.** Every other headline rule (word count, outcome-first, believable numbers) is downstream of "can a distracted human absorb this without trying?"
2. **"You will always attract below the level that you speak to."** This quietly explains why pages fail even with good copy — the calibration of your numbers IS the targeting. Write for one tier above your real buyer.
3. **Proof placement beats proof quantity.** He puts the dashboard screenshot BEFORE the story, curates 20 of 850 testimonials, and warns that too much proof "feels like overcompensating." Selection is the skill, volume is the crutch.
4. **The testimonial-level rule cuts hard for a beginner audience:** show believable first wins, not jackpot stories — "display zero-to-heroes and you will attract zeros" also means jackpot proof attracts buyers who expect jackpots and refund when they don't hit one.
5. **Middle of funnel is for consumption, not conversion.** "Seven hours of content, not convert, not convert" — the discipline to put NO buy button on retargeting content is what makes the one buy button work.
6. **The complexity ladder is the most honest thing in the course.** A $27 self-liquidating offer is "a 10 out of 10" difficulty. The order is: dial in the simple funnel (content → DM → close, a 2/10) until it's boring, THEN build the page machine. Your DM skills are the 2/10 you've already dialed — that's the right foundation, not a detour.
7. **Design is argument, not decoration.** Red = pain, green = goal, 10 cramped steps vs 4 clean ones — his graphics persuade people who never read a word. Whitespace is a claim ("this is simpler").
8. **"The actual result is senior to anything."** He killed his own favorite angle ("2x your ROAS") because it lost to the control. The willingness to delete what you love is the difference between a page that's clever and a page that's rich.
