# DM Closer — Ready-to-Post Reels: Digital Marketing & Launch

The companion set to the closing reels — these cover the **Offer, Launch,
Traffic/Hooks, and Story** pillars so your feed teaches real digital-marketing
value, not only closing. Closing stays the **wedge** (every reel ties back to it +
ends with `CLOSER`), but the value here is marketing & launching digital products.

Voice: calm, low, direct, from the hip. Serve, don't chase. **No income or earnings
claims** — teach the move, show the process, never the money. Audience: course &
digital-product creators.

> **On-screen text** = what to put on screen (one line per beat, ~1.5–2s).
> **Sound** = a *small* trending audio (under ~10K uses). Reels 1–4 need **no filming**.

## Reel 2 — LAUNCH · The 5 days before you open cart (listicle · no filming)

**On-screen text:**
1. What to post the 5 days before you launch 👇
2. Day 1 — The problem. Name it sharper than they can.
3. Day 2 — The proof. Show the process working (no hype).
4. Day 3 — The objection. Handle the big one out loud.
5. Day 4 — The offer. Say exactly what it is.
6. Day 5 — Open. Two-option CTA, never "let me know."
7. Then close the DMs it brings in.

**Caption:** A launch isn't a day — it's a runway. Five days of content that warms people up, handles the doubt, and makes the open obvious. Then it comes down to the DMs. Comment **CLOSER** for the close-it-out scripts. Save this for your next launch.

**Why:** Launch-pillar framework; ends on the close; "Day 1–5" structure is screenshot-worthy.

## Reel 4 — TRAFFIC/HOOKS · Why your reels flop in 3 seconds (one idea · text or talking head)

**Hook / line 1:** "Your reel isn't bad. Your first 3 seconds are."

**Body (beats or spoken):**
1. No movement → they scroll. Add motion in the first frame.
2. No text hook → they don't know why to stay. Put the promise on screen.
3. Vague hook → no curiosity. Be specific and name the result.
4. Fix the open. The rest of the reel finally gets a chance.

**Caption:** Most reels don't fail in the middle — they die in the first 3 seconds. Movement, a text hook, a specific promise. Get those right and the algorithm gives the rest a shot. Comment **CLOSER** for the system that closes the people who do stick around. Save · Follow.

**Why:** Traffic-pillar tip, meta and broadly useful (high reach); wedge in the last line.

## Reel 5 — STORY/AUTHORITY · Followers don't buy (one idea · talking head or text)

**Hook / line 1:** "Your follower count isn't why people don't buy."

**Body:**
1. People don't buy because you have 50K followers.
2. They buy because you named their problem better than they could.
3. And because you made the next step feel safe.
4. You can do both with 600 followers. I did.
5. Reach gets attention. Trust + a calm close gets the sale.

**Caption:** I had 600 followers for a long time. The accounts that sell aren't the biggest — they're the clearest and the calmest in the DMs. Build trust, then close without chasing. Comment **CLOSER** for how I do the second part. Save · Follow.

**Why:** Story/authority + reframes the "I'm too small" objection; ends on the wedge.

## Reel 6 — LAUNCH/NURTURE · Stop launching to strangers (one idea · text or talking head)

**Hook / line 1:** "Stop launching to strangers. It's why your cart stays quiet."

**Body:**
1. Cold audience + launch day = crickets.
2. Warm them first: 1 week of content on the exact problem.
3. Slide into the DMs *before* you sell — start the conversation.
4. By open day, you're closing friends, not pitching strangers.

**Caption:** Most "failed launches" launched to people who weren't ready. Warm them with content, start real conversations in the DMs, *then* open. The sale is just the calm continuation of a chat you already started. Comment **CLOSER** for the DM side of this. Save this.

**Why:** Launch/nurture value that leads straight into the DM close — your wedge, end to end.
