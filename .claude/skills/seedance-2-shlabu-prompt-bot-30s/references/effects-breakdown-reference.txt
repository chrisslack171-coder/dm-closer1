EFFECTS BREAKDOWN REFERENCE — SEEDANCE 2.0 / 30 SECOND EDITION
Calibration document. Read before writing any prompt.
Purpose: set the DETAIL LEVEL and the LANGUAGE. Do not copy the content of the
examples — copy the granularity, the naming precision, and the structure.


================================================================================
PART A — EFFECT NAMING VOCABULARY
================================================================================

Name every effect with a modifier. The bare noun is never enough.

SPEED MANIPULATION
  speed ramp (acceleration)          — real-time into fast, motion blur increases
  speed ramp (deceleration)          — fast into slow, blur bleeds off
  ramp-in / ramp-out pair            — accelerate then decelerate across one beat
  sustained slow motion              — always state a percentage, e.g. "approx. 20-25% speed"
  frozen beat / near-stop            — approx. 5% speed, 8-15 frames, then release
  time-stutter (step framing)        — motion drops to discrete steps, 3-5 steps per second
  real-time reset                    — deliberate return to 100% speed; a breathing moment

CAMERA MOVEMENT
  push-in (dolly)                    — physical approach, parallax present
  digital zoom (scale-in)            — frame scales, no parallax
  pull-back reveal                   — subject stays sized, world opens behind
  orbit / arc (state direction + degrees, e.g. "clockwise arc, approx. 90°")
  whip pan (state direction + smear length, e.g. "left-to-right, 6-8 frames of smear")
  handheld drift                     — low-amplitude float, approx. 1-2% frame movement
  locked-off                         — dead still; use it as contrast, not as a default
  crane / boom (rise or descend, state the vertical distance in body-heights)
  low-angle tracking                 — camera below eyeline, moving with subject
  frame rotation (state degrees + direction, e.g. "clockwise, approx. 15-20°")

DIGITAL / OPTICAL
  motion blur smear                  — directional streak on fast movement
  lens flare (anamorphic streak / circular ghost — say which)
  bloom bleed                        — highlights blow out and spill into midtones
  chromatic fringing                 — colour separation at frame edges under stress
  light wrap                         — background light bleeding around subject edge
  atmospheric haze / god rays        — volumetric light through dust, mist, or steam
  particle wash                      — dust, sand, spray, embers crossing frame
  rack focus (state from-what to-what)
  shallow depth (state approximate stop feel, e.g. "T1.5-look, background dissolved")
  grain lift                         — texture increases in the darker segment

TRANSITIONS (these are shots, not connectors)
  whip-pan transition                — motion smear carries across a cut
  light-flash / bloom transition     — highlight blows to white, next shot resolves out of it
  match-cut on shape or motion (name the shape or the vector being matched)
  smear dissolve                     — outgoing frame streaks in the direction of travel
  cut-on-impact                      — hard cut landing on a physical contact beat
  through-object wipe                — camera passes an obstruction that wipes the frame

COMPOSITING
  world-space element                — locked to the scene, moves with camera parallax
  screen-space element               — locked to the frame, ignores camera
  contact shadow                     — grounding shadow where element meets surface
  reflection pickup                  — element appearing in wet ground, glass, or metal

PART B — WORKED EXAMPLE (MULTI-SHOT, 28 SECONDS)
================================================================================
Brief that produced it: "Trail running shoe brand film. Mountain ridge, golden
hour, one runner. Epic but restrained. About 30 seconds."


SECTION 1: EFFECTS TIMELINE

SHOT 1 / MOMENT (0.0s - 3.5s) — Cold Open, Ridge Silhouette
• EFFECT: locked-off wide + atmospheric haze (god rays) + grain lift
• Wide static frame. A ridge line cuts the frame diagonally from lower-left to
  upper-right. Sun sits just above the ridge, backlighting a thin band of
  airborne dust. The runner is a small silhouette entering from frame left,
  occupying roughly 4% of frame height.
• Camera is dead still. Long lens compression, ridge and sun stacked flat.
• Real-time, 100% speed. No movement in the frame except the runner and the dust.
• Holds. The stillness is the setup — everything after this moves.

SHOT 2 / MOMENT (3.5s - 7.0s) — Low-Angle Contact
• EFFECT: low-angle tracking + speed ramp (deceleration) + particle wash
• Camera drops to ground level, roughly 20cm off the trail, tracking with the
  runner's feet moving right to left. Grit and small stones kick toward lens.
• Wide lens, close to the shoe. Foot strike fills lower third of frame.
• Enters at 100% speed, ramps down to approximately 25% speed across 12-15
  frames as the shoe compresses into the dirt, then holds slow through the
  toe-off.
• Releases back toward real time as the foot leaves frame — the release carries
  the momentum into the next shot.

SHOT 3 / MOMENT (7.0s - 7.5s) — Whip Transition
• EFFECT: whip pan (left-to-right) + motion blur smear + chromatic fringing
• The frame smears horizontally, ridge and sky reducing to colour bands. Warm
  highlights streak longest, shadows shortest. Slight colour separation at the
  left and right frame edges under the speed.
• Camera rotates fast on its vertical axis, approximately 6-8 frames of full smear.
• Faster than real time, roughly 200% through the smear.
• Resolves out of the smear directly into Shot 4 already in motion.

SHOT 4 / MOMENT (7.5s - 13.0s) — Ridge Run, Orbit
• EFFECT: clockwise orbit (approx. 120°) + shallow depth + anamorphic lens flare
• Runner on the ridge crest, camera arcing around behind them as they move.
  Valley opens and closes behind the subject as the arc travels. Sun enters
  frame at approximately 10.5s and throws a horizontal anamorphic streak across
  the upper third.
• Mid-length lens, T1.5-look — the valley is dissolved into soft warm gradient.
• Real-time, 100% speed. Deliberate reset after the ramp in Shot 2.
• Arc slows as it completes; the deceleration of the camera hands off to the
  pull-back in Shot 5.

SHOT 5 / MOMENT (13.0s - 18.0s) — Scale Reveal
• EFFECT: pull-back reveal (crane, descend-to-rise) + atmospheric haze +
  bloom bleed
• Camera pulls back and rises approximately six body-heights. The runner stays
  roughly the same size in frame while the mountain range unfolds behind and
  below. Layered haze separates each ridge into its own tonal band.
• Wide lens, camera moving continuously, no cut.
• Real-time throughout. Movement is slow and even — no easing spikes.
• Highlights on the furthest ridge bloom and bleed into the sky at around 17s,
  carrying the frame toward white for the transition.

SHOT 6 / MOMENT (18.0s - 18.4s) — Bloom Flash
• EFFECT: light-flash transition (bloom to near-white) + light wrap
• The blown highlight expands to fill roughly 80% of frame. The runner's edge
  holds as a dark line wrapped in warm light for 3-4 frames before the frame
  clears.
• No camera movement of its own — the flash carries the cut.
• Approximately 10 frames total.
• The next shot resolves out of the white rather than cutting into it.

SHOT 7 / MOMENT (18.4s - 24.0s) — Descent, Signature Beat
• EFFECT: speed ramp (deceleration) + frozen beat + particle wash + frame
  rotation (clockwise, approx. 15-20°)
• THIS IS THE SIGNATURE VISUAL EFFECT. Runner leaves the ridge into a loose
  scree descent. As the lead foot lands, spray of dust and small stone fans
  outward in a full arc. Motion ramps from 100% down to approximately 20%
  speed, then to a near-stop of approximately 5% for 10-12 frames at maximum
  particle spread. Through the near-stop, the frame rotates clockwise 15-20°,
  putting the slope on a diagonal. Particles hang, individually resolved.
• Camera is close and low, moving with the descent, roughly 1.5m from the subject.
• Release from the near-stop is fast — back to 100% within 8 frames.
• The rotation unwinds as speed returns, levelling by the end of the block.

SHOT 8 / MOMENT (24.0s - 28.0s) — Resolution
• EFFECT: handheld drift + rack focus (foreground grass to runner) + grain lift
• Runner slows to a walk on the flat, back to camera, valley ahead. Foreground
  grass in the near field. Focus starts on the grass and racks to the runner
  across approximately 1.2 seconds at 25.0s.
• Handheld float, low amplitude — approximately 1-2% frame movement, no obvious
  drift direction.
• Real-time, 100% speed for the full block.
• Ends on the hold. Light drops perceptibly across the last second as the sun
  clears the ridge behind camera. Grain lifts as the exposure falls.


SECTION 2: MASTER EFFECTS INVENTORY

SPEED MANIPULATION
1. Speed ramp (deceleration) — used 2x — Shots 2, 7 — drops the viewer into
   physical detail at the two contact beats.
2. Frozen beat / near-stop — used 1x — Shot 7 — holds the particle spread at
   maximum extension; the anchor of the whole film.
3. Real-time reset — used 3x — Shots 4, 5, 8 — protects the film from ramp
   fatigue; every slow beat is paid for by a real-time stretch.

CAMERA MOVEMENT
4. Locked-off wide — used 1x — Shot 1 — stillness as the baseline the rest
   departs from.
5. Low-angle tracking — used 1x — Shot 2 — puts the product at eye level with
   the ground.
6. Clockwise orbit (approx. 120°) — used 1x — Shot 4 — reveals the valley
   without cutting.
7. Pull-back reveal (crane) — used 1x — Shot 5 — establishes scale at the
   midpoint.
8. Handheld drift — used 1x — Shot 8 — returns human, unstable framing for the
   landing.
9. Frame rotation (clockwise, 15-20°) — used 1x — Shot 7 — destabilises the
   signature beat.

TRANSITIONS
10. Whip pan + smear — used 1x — Shot 3 — carries momentum out of the first
    slow beat.
11. Light-flash / bloom transition — used 1x — Shot 6 — resets the eye before
    the signature beat.

OPTICAL / ATMOSPHERIC
12. Atmospheric haze / god rays — used 2x — Shots 1, 5 — separates depth
    layers, warms the grade.
13. Particle wash — used 2x — Shots 2, 7 — the physical texture of the surface.
14. Anamorphic lens flare — used 1x — Shot 4 — single warm streak, not repeated.
15. Bloom bleed — used 1x — Shot 5 — sets up the flash transition.
16. Light wrap — used 1x — Shot 6 — holds the subject readable through the flash.
17. Chromatic fringing — used 1x — Shot 3 — edge stress under the whip only.
18. Shallow depth (T1.5-look) — used 1x — Shot 4 — isolates the runner on the ridge.
19. Rack focus — used 1x — Shot 8 — the last movement in the film.
20. Grain lift — used 2x — Shots 1, 8 — bookends the film with texture.


SECTION 3: EFFECTS DENSITY MAP

0.0s - 3.5s   = LOW DENSITY (locked-off, haze, grain — 3 effects in 3.5s, all static)
3.5s - 7.0s   = MEDIUM DENSITY (low-angle track, deceleration ramp, particle wash — 3 effects in 3.5s)
7.0s - 7.5s   = HIGH DENSITY (whip pan, motion smear, chromatic fringing, 200% speed — 4 effects in 0.5s)
7.5s - 13.0s  = MEDIUM DENSITY (orbit, shallow depth, anamorphic flare — 3 effects in 5.5s)
13.0s - 18.0s = LOW DENSITY (crane pull-back, haze, bloom — 3 effects in 5.0s, one continuous move)
18.0s - 18.4s = HIGH DENSITY (bloom flash, light wrap, near-white blowout — 3 effects in 0.4s)
18.4s - 24.0s = HIGH DENSITY (deceleration ramp, near-stop, particle wash, frame rotation, fast release — 5 effects in 5.6s)
24.0s - 28.0s = LOW DENSITY (handheld drift, rack focus, grain lift — 3 effects in 4.0s)

Note the shape: the two HIGH stretches sit at 7.0s and 18.4s, and the second is
paid for by the LOW stretch at 13.0s-18.0s immediately before it. The film never
runs two HIGH segments back to back.


SECTION 4: MOTION FLOW

OPENING (0.0s - 7.0s)
Enters still. The first thing the viewer feels is scale and quiet — a locked
frame with one small moving element. The drop to ground level at 3.5s is the
first physical jolt, and the deceleration ramp on the foot strike is the first
time the film asks the viewer to look closely at something specific. Energy
climbs from zero.

BUILD, FIRST MOVEMENT (7.0s - 13.0s)
The whip breaks the observational mode. From here the camera is a participant,
not a witness. The orbit runs at real time deliberately — after a ramp, the
honest speed reads as confidence rather than as a gap.

RESET (13.0s - 18.0s)
The breathing moment the extended duration requires. One continuous camera move,
no speed manipulation, no cuts. Scale replaces speed as the source of impact.
This stretch exists so the signature beat lands.

PEAK (18.0s - 24.0s)
The flash clears the eye, then the descent delivers the signature beat: ramp
down, near-stop at maximum particle spread, rotation destabilising the horizon,
then a fast release. This is the highest energy in the film and it is the only
place where four effects stack simultaneously.

RESOLUTION (24.0s - 28.0s)
Energy lands rather than stops. Handheld float returns a human hand to the
camera, the rack focus gives the eye one last deliberate movement, and the
falling light closes the frame. The final impression is aftermath, not arrival.

PART C — WORKED EXCERPT (SINGLE CONTINUOUS SHOT, 24 SECONDS)
================================================================================
Shows how to structure Section 1 when there are no cuts. Same detail level;
transitions become internal momentum shifts.
Brief that produced it: "One unbroken shot through a rain-wet city street at
night, following a courier on foot. Neon. No cuts."


SECTION 1: EFFECTS TIMELINE — SINGLE CONTINUOUS SHOT, NO CUTS

MOMENT (0.0s) — Entry
• EFFECT: through-object wipe + shallow depth + reflection pickup
• Camera starts behind a rain-streaked window, then passes through the open
  doorway into the street. The window edge wipes the frame left to right as the
  camera clears it. Wet pavement carries inverted neon.
• Handheld, chest height, moving forward at walking pace.
• Real-time, 100% speed.
• The wipe hands the frame to the street — no cut, the obstruction does the work.

MOMENT (3.0s - 8.0s) — Pickup and Follow
• EFFECT: push-in (dolly) + rack focus (street depth to courier) + light wrap
• Courier enters from frame right ahead of camera. Focus rides from the far
  street to the courier's back across roughly 0.8 seconds. Neon signage behind
  them wraps a magenta edge around their shoulders.
• Camera accelerates from walking to jogging pace, closing distance by about
  half.
• Real-time. Handheld amplitude rises with pace — approximately 3% frame movement.
• Momentum shift, not a cut: the pace increase is the transition.

MOMENT (8.0s - 12.5s) — Corner
• EFFECT: arc (left, approx. 70°) + motion blur smear + particle wash (rain)
• Courier turns hard left at a corner; camera arcs with them, the near building
  edge sweeping across frame in blur. Rain crosses the lens light from the
  upper-left.
• Camera stays roughly 2m behind, arcing on the same radius as the subject.
• Brief acceleration to approximately 130% through the turn, back to 100% on exit.
• The building edge functions as an internal wipe — the frame is fully occluded
  for 4-5 frames.

MOMENT (12.5s - 18.0s) — Underpass Reset
• EFFECT: real-time reset + grain lift + locked framing distance
• Light drops as the courier enters an underpass. Neon falls away; a single
  sodium fixture carries the exposure. Grain rises noticeably. Camera holds a
  constant distance — no push, no pull.
• Handheld amplitude drops back to approximately 1%.
• Real-time, 100% speed, no manipulation of any kind for the full block.
• This is the breathing stretch. It exists to make the exit beat land.

MOMENT (18.0s - 24.0s) — Exit
• EFFECT: speed ramp (deceleration) + bloom bleed + anamorphic lens flare +
  frozen beat
• THIS IS THE SIGNATURE VISUAL EFFECT. The courier exits the underpass into
  full neon. Headlights of a passing car enter frame right and bloom hard.
  Motion ramps from 100% to approximately 20% speed as the courier crosses the
  light, holding a near-stop of approximately 5% for 12 frames with rain
  suspended mid-fall around them, each drop individually lit.
• Camera continues forward through the ramp — the move never stops, only the
  world slows.
• Release back to 100% across 10 frames, then the courier pulls ahead and the
  camera lets the distance open.
• Ends with the camera slowing to a stop as the courier leaves frame.

PART D — WORKED EXAMPLE (COMPOSITE MODE)
================================================================================
Brief that produced it: "Here's my existing 12s clip of a car on a coast road.
Keep everything the same, just add a drone flying alongside it." Video file
supplied plus one product render.


📎 IMAGE/VIDEO REFERENCE MAP
• Video 1 (coast_road_v3.mp4) → base footage, retained exactly as-is
• Image 1 (drone_render_white.png) → element being composited in (product)


COMPOSITE BRIEF

The base footage is a 12-second aerial-adjacent tracking shot of a dark car
travelling right to left on a coastal road, late afternoon, sun low and behind
camera-left. Every frame of the existing footage is retained exactly as shot —
no regrade, no reframe, no change to the car, road, sea, or camera path.

Composite in the white quadcopter drone from the reference render as a
world-space element, flying alongside and slightly ahead of the car, offset
approximately 4 metres to the seaward side and 2.5 metres above road level. It
holds that offset relative to the car for the full duration, so it inherits the
base footage's camera parallax exactly — as the camera moves, the drone shifts
against the background at the same rate as the car, not at the rate of the
frame.

The drone is present from frame one. No entry animation.

Light integration: key from camera-left and slightly behind, matching the
existing sun position. The upper-left surfaces of the drone's body and arms
carry a warm specular hit; the underside sits in cool bounce picked up from the
sea below, noticeably bluer than the topside. A faint warm rim runs along the
seaward edge. The white body picks up a subtle gold cast overall to sit in the
same grade as the road surface.

Shadow: no ground contact shadow — the drone is airborne over open water for
most of the shot. Where the road passes beneath it at approximately 7s, a soft,
heavily diffused shadow crosses the tarmac, offset to camera-right of the drone
and roughly 3x its footprint, at low opacity.

Motion behaviour: rotors spin fast enough to read as motion-blurred discs, not
discrete blades. The airframe carries low-amplitude float — a 2-3° pitch and
roll drift on a slow irregular cycle, never mechanical or looping. At
approximately 8s it banks approximately 8° into the road's left curve, holding
the bank for roughly 1.5 seconds before levelling as the road straightens.

The drone stays fully in frame for the whole clip and never crosses in front of
the car.

PART E — DETAIL LEVEL CHECKLIST
================================================================================

Before delivering, verify:

□ Every effect name carries a modifier (direction, degrees, percentage, or type)
□ Every slow-motion beat has an explicit speed percentage
□ Every camera move states its type AND its extent
□ Every stacked moment lists all simultaneous effects on the EFFECT line
□ At least one moment is explicitly called out as the SIGNATURE VISUAL EFFECT
  (two to three, spaced apart, on 22-30s pieces)
□ No two HIGH DENSITY segments sit back to back
□ At least one LOW DENSITY reset exists in any piece over 22 seconds
□ Transitions are written as their own moments, with their own effects and
  frame counts
□ The description says what the frame does, not what software would do it
□ Section 4 covers opening, build, and resolution as distinct phases
□ Total duration does not exceed 30 seconds
